Aftercare

The Complete Lash Extension Aftercare Guide

Application is our half of retention. This is yours.

By The Nora Beauty Lounge Team · June 18, 2026 · Johns Creek, GA

After eleven years behind the lash bed, I can usually tell within thirty seconds of a fill how a client has cared for her set. The good news is that great aftercare is not complicated. It is a handful of small habits, done consistently, and they are the difference between a set that limps to week two and one that still turns heads at week four.

The first 24 hours

Modern adhesives cure faster than the old 48-hour rule suggests, but the bond is still settling for a full day. For the first 24 hours, keep your lashes dry — no swimming, no steamy showers, no hot yoga, no crying at weddings if you can schedule around it. Skip eye makeup entirely and resist the urge to touch. The oils on your fingertips are exactly what the adhesive dislikes most. If you walk out of our Johns Creek lounge into summer humidity, don't worry; ambient moisture is fine. It is direct water, heat, and oil that interfere with a curing bond.

Cleansing: the habit that decides everything

Here is the counterintuitive part. The clients with the worst retention are usually not the ones who wash their lashes too much — they are the ones too scared to wash them at all. Skipped cleansing lets oil, makeup residue, and skin debris accumulate at the lash line, where they slowly degrade the adhesive and, worse, can inflame the follicles. Every case of lash-line irritation I have seen in this studio traces back to under-cleansing.

Once a day, ideally in the evening, lather an oil-free lash shampoo with a soft cleansing brush and work it gently along the lash line with your eyes closed. Rinse with lukewarm water, press — never rub — with a lint-free towel, and let them air dry or use a handheld fan on cool. Then brush them into place with a clean spoolie. The whole ritual takes ninety seconds. Every new set at Nora Beauty Lounge includes an aftercare kit with the correct shampoo, brush, and spoolie, so there is nothing to source on your own.

Brushing, sleeping, and the small mechanics

Brush your lashes each morning with a dry spoolie, from mid-length to tip — not from the base, where you can catch and twist the bond. Two passes is plenty; lashes are not hair that needs a hundred strokes.

Sleep is where sets quietly die. Stomach sleepers grind their extensions into the pillow for seven hours a night, and it shows as one eye thinning faster than the other. Train yourself toward back or side sleeping, switch to a silk or satin pillowcase so any contact slides instead of snags, and if you are a committed stomach sleeper, a contoured sleep mask with recessed eye cups is a worthwhile ten-dollar insurance policy.

What to avoid

Oil is the recurring villain of this story. Oil-based makeup removers, cleansing balms, heavy facial oils, and rich eye creams all migrate — you apply them an inch from your lash line and by morning they have found the adhesive. Keep oils below the cheekbone and use oil-free formulas around the eyes. Waterproof mascara deserves a special mention: it should not be worn over extensions at all, and if you feel your set needs mascara, that is a design conversation for your next appointment, not a product problem. Avoid lash curlers entirely — mechanical curlers snap extensions at the bond — and skip at-home lash serums with prostaglandin analogs unless we have talked about them, because they change your growth cycle in ways that affect fill planning.

Steam rooms, saunas, and tanning beds weaken adhesive over time. You do not have to give them up; just know that a weekly sauna habit means booking fills on the earlier side of the two-to-three-week window. Our fill pricing is built around that cadence.

Products worth keeping on the shelf

You need very little: an oil-free foaming lash shampoo, soft cleansing brushes you replace monthly, clean spoolies, a lint-free towel or lint-free applicator pads, and a silk pillowcase. If you wear eye makeup, choose oil-free micellar water on a pad, pressed gently, never dragged. That is the entire list. Anything more elaborate being marketed to you is solving a problem you do not have.

Aftercare is not about being precious with your lashes — it is about removing the four or five things that sabotage them. Do the daily cleanse, sleep kindly, keep oil away, and your set will reward you at every fill.

Quick answers

When can I shower normally after getting extensions?

After 24 hours. Even then, avoid aiming hot water directly at your closed eyes for long stretches — let the water hit your face briefly, cleanse, and move on. Lukewarm is kinder to adhesive than hot.

How often should I wash lash extensions?

Once daily with an oil-free lash shampoo, and always after workouts, swimming, or heavy makeup days. Under-washing, not over-washing, is the more common cause of poor retention and lash-line irritation.

What if my eyes feel irritated after a new set?

Mild watering on day one can be normal. Redness, swelling, or itching that persists past 24 hours is not — contact the studio promptly. We will assess it, and if needed, perform a gentle removal at no charge. More questions are answered on our FAQ page.
